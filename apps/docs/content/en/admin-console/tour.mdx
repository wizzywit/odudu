# A first visit to the console

You have Odudu running and one administrator. In about ten minutes you will sign in, step into a tenant, learn what each part of the menu is for, open one person's record and find the entry in the audit trail that says who made it. Nothing here changes anything, so you can follow it on any installation.

If you have not started Odudu yet, begin with the [quick start](../quick-start/index.mdx). It leaves you with a tenant called `demo`, a client called `my-app` and a person called `ada`, which is what the pictures below show.

## 1. Say which tenant you administer

Open `http://localhost:3000/console/` in your browser. The console first asks **Which tenant do you administer?**. Type `system`, the tenant your first administrator belongs to, and choose **Continue to sign-in**.

## 2. Sign in

You land on Odudu's own sign-in page, the one every person of that tenant meets. Enter the administrator's email as the **Username** and the **Password** you were given.

<ConsoleShot
  name="sign-in"
  alt="Odudu's sign-in page for the system tenant, with Username and Password fields."
  caption="Odudu's own sign-in page, with the administrator's email as the username."
/>

The console never sees your password. It asks Odudu to sign you in, keeps the tokens it gets back on the server, and gives your browser a session cookie.

## 3. See the tenants

You arrive on **Tenants**, the first area of the **SYSTEM** group of the menu. It lists every tenant of this installation. An administrator of the system tenant is the only one who sees it.

<ConsoleShot
  name="tenants"
  alt="The Tenants list of the system tenant: system and demo, each with an Enter button, and the Import a tenant and Create a tenant buttons."
  caption="The system tenant lists every tenant, with a way into each."
/>

The [Tenants](./tenants.mdx) page covers creating, exporting and deleting them. An administrator of any other tenant lands in that tenant's own **Overview** instead, with no list to choose from.

## 4. Enter a tenant

Choose **Enter demo**. The console opens the tenant's **Overview** and shows a banner that says you are acting there with system authority, with **Back to system** beside it when you want to leave.

<ConsoleShot
  name="overview"
  alt="The demo tenant's Overview: nothing needs attention, the counts of its subjects, clients, groups, roles and scopes, and its discovery addresses, with the console's menu on the left."
  caption="A tenant opens on its Overview: what needs attention, the counts and its addresses."
/>

The Overview answers two questions, whether anything needs your attention and how big the tenant is. Each count is a link to the list it counts. [Overview](./overview.mdx) goes through it.

## 5. Read the menu

The menu on the left is the whole console. It groups the areas by what you are trying to do.

- **IDENTITY** holds the people: [Subjects](./subjects.mdx), [Groups](./groups.mdx) and [Roles](./roles.mdx).
- **APPLICATIONS** holds what signs them in: [Clients](./clients.mdx) (whose **Branding** tab is [Client branding](./client-branding.mdx)), [Scopes](./scopes.mdx) and [Registration tokens](./registration-tokens.mdx).
- **SECURITY** holds how they are checked: [Sign-in flow](./sign-in-flow.mdx), [Signing keys](./signing-keys.mdx) and [Sessions](./sessions.mdx).
- **TENANT** holds the tenant itself: [Settings](./settings.mdx), [Branding](./branding.mdx), [Email](./email.mdx) and Export, which is on the [Tenants](./tenants.mdx) page.
- **OBSERVE** holds the record: [Audit trail](./audit-trail.mdx).

The menu lists only the areas you may read, so an administrator with fewer capabilities sees a shorter menu. An address to an area you may not read still opens, and says which capability it needs. Each page of this section names that capability.

## 6. Open one person

Choose **Subjects**, then `ada`. Her record has a tab for each part of an account, from **Profile** to **Activity**, and nothing on it changes until you press a button to save.

## 7. Find who made a change

Choose **Audit trail**. Every change made in the tenant is there, newest first, with who made it. The three rows below are the tenant, the client and the person from the quick start, each made by "a system administrator".

## 8. Sign out

Choose **Sign out** at the bottom of the menu, beside the name you are signed in as. The console revokes the refresh token it held for you, clears its cookie and ends your sign-in with the tenant too, and you arrive at the sign-in page. The console's own client skips the sign-out confirmation, so nothing asks first.

## If a page stops working

Now and then a page fails to draw. The console replaces that page with a panel that says **Something went wrong**, and leaves the menu where it is, so you can go on elsewhere. Changes you had saved are kept; changes you had not saved on the page that failed cannot be recovered. **Try again** draws the page once more. It keeps you where you are, so it does not ask anything. **Back to overview** takes you to the tenant's overview and **Reload the console** starts the console over; both leave the page, so they ask first if another form still open holds changes you have not saved.

Under the buttons is a short block with an **Error ID**, the page's address with its names left out, and the time. Choose **Copy** and send it to whoever runs your Odudu. The ID is made in your browser, so it is not in the server's logs. To find the error, whoever runs your Odudu needs the full error shown beside the same ID in the browser's developer console, so copy that too. The panel never shows what the error said, and the block holds nothing about you.

If the failure is in the menu itself, you get the same panel alone on the page, with the same three buttons. If it is in the console as a whole, you get that panel with a single **Reload the console** button.

A failure that happens when you press a button, rather than while a page draws, shows no panel. The button simply does nothing and the page stays as it was; the browser's developer console holds the error. Reload if it keeps happening.

## What the console leaves to the command line

The console covers what an administrator does day to day. Four operator commands have no button. `odudu reap`, `odudu send-mail` and `odudu send-logouts` are passes the server already runs on its own schedule, which a deployment may turn off and run itself, so the console shows what some of them produce and does not start them. `odudu client-origins rebuild` is run once, after an upgrade that adds client origins. The [roadmap](../roadmap.md) says why.
