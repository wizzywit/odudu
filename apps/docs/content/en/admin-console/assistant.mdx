# The assistant in the console

The assistant is a panel that opens beside any page of the console. You ask a question in plain words, and it reads what it needs from the tenant, as you could, and answers. It is not an area with a menu entry. It needs the `use-assistant` capability. A system administrator who is looking at another tenant has it there too, in Ask mode only, when they also hold `manage-tenants` ([below](#4-in-another-tenant)).

The button is **Ask the assistant**, in the menu and, on a narrow screen, in the top bar, where it reads **Ask** to leave room for the name of the tenant and the menu button. It is drawn only where the server has the assistant switched on, the tenant offers it and you may use it. Where any of those is not true there is no button, no shortcut and nothing asked of the server. The tenant's own switch and limits are in [Settings](./settings.mdx), under **Console assistant**.

## 1. Open it

Choose **Ask the assistant**, or press Command and K on a Mac, Control and K elsewhere. The panel takes the focus to its heading, so a screen reader starts at the top, and the message field is one Tab away. Escape or **Close** puts the panel away and the focus goes back to whatever opened it. Putting it away does not end an answer that is being written: the answer goes on, and is there when you open the panel again. Opened again, it comes back to the conversation you had, and after a reload it reopens your latest one with its messages ([section 7](#7-your-conversations)). **New conversation**, under the title, starts one when no answer is being written.

Where the console is wide enough, the panel has a column of its own beside the page and stays there as you move between pages. Where it is not, the panel lies over the page as a dialog: the page behind it is out of reach, Tab stays inside it, and on a phone it fills the screen.

The first time you use it in a browser, a notice says where your messages go and who can read the conversation. It names the provider that receives your messages and the tenant data the assistant reads to answer them, or, for a model your operator runs, the host that does. It says that you can read the conversation again later, and that people who can read the tenant's audit trail can read it too unless an administrator has turned that off in Settings. Choose **Got it** and it does not come back in that browser. Where a browser cannot remember that, the notice shows again.

## 2. Ask a question

Type in the message field and press Enter. Shift and Enter start a new line. Next to the field a chip says where you are, such as `client my-app` on a client's page: that is sent with your first message, as the address of the page and, on a page of one record, its kind and id, and never as text you typed. The assistant can read what you can read there: clients, subjects, roles, settings and the audit trail.

The conversation starts with your first message, not when the panel opens, so opening it and closing it again costs nothing. Your words appear as you typed them. The answer appears as it is written, with emphasis, lists, code and tables. A table that is wider than the panel scrolls sideways inside the answer, with the arrow keys once you have tabbed to it, and the page does not move. A link to a console page stays a link; any other address is shown as text, and an image is not shown, since an answer can carry text a tenant wrote.

## 3. Ask and Act

**Ask** is the mode: the assistant reads and explains, and changes nothing. **Act** lets it propose changes, each waiting for your approval, and needs the approval card, which arrives next. Until then the **Mode** switch shows Act dimmed with a sentence that says so, and every conversation starts in Ask, whatever the tenant's setting says. A conversation that was already in Act, such as one made through the API, shows **Waiting for you** and takes no new message here.

## 4. In another tenant

A system administrator who has opened another tenant, such as `demo` while signed in to `system`, sees **Ask the assistant** there when they hold both `use-assistant` and `manage-tenants`, the server has the assistant on, and `demo` has not switched it off. The tenant's own administrators are unaffected, and an administrator without `manage-tenants` is offered nothing in another tenant.

The panel says where it acts for as long as it is open, in a tag at the top: `Acting in demo`. The assistant reads what you can read in that tenant, with your own authority, which the server checks on every call, so a role removed from you in the middle of a conversation takes effect on the next step. It makes no change: **Act** is dimmed with **Act is not available in another tenant.**, and every conversation starts in Ask.

A few things differ from your own tenant, and the first-run notice for another tenant, which is remembered apart from the other one, says them.

- Your own tenant pays. The conversation, its allowance and its record belong to your own tenant. The tenant's switch, its masking of email addresses and its Act setting apply as well as your tenant's, and the stricter of the two wins.
- They see actions, not text. The administrators of `demo` can see in their audit trail that you used the assistant there and which tools it ran, and never what you wrote or what it answered.
- The conversation is separate. A conversation made in `demo` is not offered in `system`, or in another tenant.

If the assistant cannot be used there, the panel says why in plain words. The tenant, or your own, has switched it off: “The assistant is not available in demo. demo may have switched it off, or your own tenant has.” You no longer hold what the server asks: “You need use-assistant and manage-tenants to use the assistant in demo. Ask an administrator of your own tenant.” A tool the server refuses mid-answer is listed in the answer as **Refused**.

## 5. Watch it work

Under the conversation one short line says what the assistant is doing: **Connecting** while the server takes your message, **Thinking** while it works out an answer, the step it is on while it reads (**Listing clients**), **Waiting for you**, **Stopped** or **Finished**. A failure is shown in a notice of its own, not on that line. A screen reader is told when that line changes, and never hears the words of the answer as they are written. Each step the assistant took is listed in its answer with what became of it.

**Stop** takes the place of **Send** for as long as an answer is being written, and the message field keeps the focus, so you can go on typing the next question. Choosing **Stop** ends the answer here and on the server, which stops the spend, and says so: **Stopped. Nothing further was run.** Stop is the only thing that ends an answer: putting the panel away does not, and leaving the console does.

## 6. Read why it stopped

An answer ends in one of a few ways, and the panel names which.

- **Finished.** The answer is complete.
- **Stopped at the step limit.** One answer may take only so many model and tool calls, which [Settings](./settings.mdx) names. Ask again, with a narrower question, to carry on.
- **Stopped: allowance used.** The assistant has four limits: the tenant's month for the model, your share of one day, one conversation's month and one answer. The panel names the one that was reached, in the server's words, which say what resets it and who can change it. The assistant never falls back to a cheaper model. A conversation or an answer that reached its own limit has no setting; start a new conversation or ask a narrower question.
- **Stopped**, with a note that the connection closed before the answer finished. The stream ended without saying how the answer ended, as when a proxy cut it. What was written is kept, and nothing is claimed as finished.
- **Failed.** The panel says in plain words what could not be done, and gives the request's own reference to copy when it has one, for whoever looks into it. It never shows an error's stack.

The panel says nothing about your allowance until the server does. When a limit passes 80 percent during an answer, a line at the top says how much of which limit is used and what happens at the limit, and when one is reached it names it. Both stay until an answer ends well. The server sends the share at which it warned and not a running count, so the panel shows no more than that.

## 7. Your conversations

**Conversations**, under the title, shows every conversation of yours in this tenant, newest first, under **Today**, **Yesterday**, **Earlier this week** and **Earlier**. The list takes the panel's whole height and slides in over the conversation, which stays where it was, so an answer that is being written goes on behind it. **Back**, or Escape, returns to it. Each row is one line: the title, when the conversation was last active, its mode, and, when you are acting in another tenant, the tenant's name. The conversation that is open is marked. A title is made from the first thing you asked, with addresses, links, long numbers and anything that looks like a secret taken out. Use the arrow keys to move through the rows, Enter to open one, and type a few letters of a title to jump to it.

A conversation belongs to one tenant: the list shows the conversations you had in the tenant you are looking at, and a system administrator acting in another tenant sees that tenant's own list.

Opening: The panel opens on your latest conversation that is not archived, with its messages, and after a reload does the same. Choose a row to open another. If an answer is still being written, a note asks whether to **Stop and switch**, which ends the answer here and on the server and then opens the one you chose, or **Keep waiting**, which leaves everything as it was. Choosing the conversation that is already open just takes you back to it.

Row actions: **Actions**, on each row, opens a menu.

- **Archive** hides the conversation at once and offers **Undo** for as long as you stay in the list. Nothing is removed. An **Archived** section at the foot of the list, with its count, holds what you hid; open one and the composer is replaced by a note and **Restore**.
- **Export as Markdown** and **Export as JSON** save the conversation as a file: its first 500 messages, with secrets hidden, and the names of the tools an answer called but not what they were given or returned. Each export is recorded in the audit trail as an action, never with the text.
- **Delete** asks first, naming the conversation. Its messages, its steps and its approval cards are removed and cannot be brought back, and the server keeps a record, without any text, that a conversation was deleted. **Delete conversation** is refused in plain words while an answer is running in it, and for a conversation that was reported, which is kept for review: you can archive it, and its menu shows **Delete** dimmed with the reason. A reported conversation carries a **Reported** tag.

A conversation that can be read but not continued: A conversation can only take a message under the sign-in it was started in. After you sign in again, an older conversation opens for reading, and in place of the message field the panel says so and offers **Start a new conversation**. If you send a message to one the server then refuses, the panel says the same, takes the message back and offers the same button. Nothing is lost: the conversation can still be read, exported and deleted. Restoring an archived conversation brings the message field back.

If the assistant is switched off meanwhile: When an administrator turns the assistant off for the tenant while a conversation is running, a tool's next call is refused and the panel says so, in a note under the conversation: the assistant was switched off, nothing further was run, and an administrator can switch it on again. What was already said is kept.

A conversation is removed after it has had no new message for the number of days the tenant sets under **Keep traces for** in [Settings](./settings.mdx), 30 by default, and shortening it applies at the next pass of `odudu reap`. The list says so in a line under it, without the number, because the number is the administrators' to read. [The assistant's trace](../operating/assistant-trace.md) has the detail.

## Things that cannot be undone

**Stop** cannot take back what has been sent. The request to the model provider is made as soon as you send a message, and what the provider has received stays with it. A step that has already run has run; in Ask mode every step only reads. The conversation is recorded, so putting the panel away does not delete it: it stays readable to you and to those the first-run notice names. Deleting it yourself, from **Conversations**, removes its text for good; archiving only hides it.

## Do the same through the API

The conversation, its messages and its stream are in the [assistant area of the admin API](../reference/generated/admin-api/assistant/index.md): starting a conversation sends the page you are on, a message is answered as a stream, a turn is stopped with its own call, and the mode is switched with another. The list, the latest conversation, hiding, exporting and deleting are routes of your own conversations that answer for you and for nobody else, and the panel's **Conversations** uses them. The console reads the stream through the same gateway as every other call, so it carries the same session and the same refusals.
