# Branding in the console

A tenant's users meet Odudu's pages when they sign in, consent, reset a password or set up a second factor. **Branding** is where the tenant makes those pages its own. It needs the `manage-tenant` capability; the menu entry is **Branding** under **TENANT**.

Nothing you change is seen until you publish it. Edits are saved as a draft, the preview shows the draft drawn by the real page, and **Publish** makes it what people see.

## 1. Set the colours and images

On the **Theme** tab, **Colours and font** holds a light palette and a dark palette. Each is used whole: turn on **Use my own light colours** and every colour in that scheme is yours, starting from Instrument's own. A scheme you leave alone keeps Instrument's colours. Under **Images**, upload a logo (with the alt text it needs), a logo for the dark scheme, a favicon and a background image. The background image is drawn behind every themed page, covering it and centred, in place of Instrument's grid; the form stays on its own panel, and the theme's stylesheet can still restyle the backdrop. A client's own background replaces the tenant's, and with none set the grid stays. PNG, JPEG and WebP are accepted, and each slot states its size limit beside it. A slot that holds an image shows it, whole and small, under its description, whether or not any published theme uses it and after a reload; if the image cannot be read the slot says so and offers to try again.

Each palette has eleven colours: Page background, Panel background, Field background, Text, Muted text, Accent background, Accent text, Error background, Error text, Border and Focus ring. Type each as `#rrggbb` or pick it. The Font is Instrument’s own, IBM Plex Sans or IBM Plex Mono, and is set for both schemes at once.

Under **Stylesheet**, write plain CSS for what the colours cannot do, such as hiding the logo or adjusting spacing. Rules may name only the pages' documented classes, and `@import`, `@font-face`, scripts and addresses outside the theme's own images are refused. The editor numbers the lines and colours the CSS; a refusal is marked on the line it names and listed under the editor, and the size left is shown beside it. Press Escape and then Tab to leave the editor with the keyboard, since Tab indents.

A theme, and the name a client is shown under, are written by the tenant's administrators: Odudu holds them to what a page can safely carry and does not judge what they mean. A template link may go to any `https` address, with `rel="noopener noreferrer"`, because where a tenant sends its people is the tenant's decision. A template is also read the way a browser reads the finished page, so a part inside a link or between table parts, and a form opened in one cell and closed in another, are refused: a browser would move them and leave the form's controls outside it. A stylesheet may hide or collapse only the decorative parts. On every other part it may not set text under 12px, boxes under 16px that clip, large margins or padding, faint or transparent text, or text the colour of its background or of the page, and it may not hide the consent page's heading or scope list, which name the client and what it asks for. `outline: none`, a zero or faint outline, one the colour of the page and `initial` or `unset` are refused on every rule, so a reset followed by a restyle is not possible: write the visible outline directly. A theme's images and stylesheet are served with `default-src 'none'; sandbox`, so opening their address runs nothing.

The **Contrast** panel checks every pair of colours as you type. Text must reach 4.5:1 against what it sits on, and borders and the focus ring 3:1. **Publish** is held while a pair falls short, and the panel names it. It checks colours against the page colour, not against the background image, so pick an image whose look suits the colours you chose. The logo, the text and the form all sit on the page's own panel, so the image shows around it and is never behind the text.

**Selectors you can use** sits above the editor and lists everything a rule may name: the page classes, one class for each kind of page (`.page-sign-in` styles the sign-in page alone), the plain elements, and the states and parts that may follow them (`:hover`, `::before`). **Design values you can use** is the second list beside it: the values a declaration may read with `var()`, which go after a colon and are refused where a selector belongs. Each entry says what it is beside its name. Type part of a name or of what it is to narrow the list, which draws at most fifty at a time, and choose one to type it where the caret is; that is one step of the editor's undo. A refused selector says to look at this list. A stylesheet cannot define a custom property of its own, and the list is the one the server checks against, so it is also the list for a client's stylesheet.

The **Contrast** panel checks every pair of colours as you type. Text must reach 4.5:1 against what it sits on, and borders and the focus ring 3:1. **Publish** is held while a pair falls short, and the panel names it.

## 2. Change a page's layout

On the **Pages** tab, choose a page and edit its template. The parts the server fills in, such as the form and its fields, are listed beside the editor; a template places them and may not add scripts, styles or forms. The template editor works as the stylesheet editor does, and a refused template says what is wrong and marks where. **Insert a message** lists the messages the page can show, with their English text beside each key, and types the reference to the one you choose at the caret; type part of a key or of its text to narrow the list, which draws at most fifty at a time.

## 3. Preview, publish, restore

The preview beside the editor frames the real page for the saved draft, in the light or the dark scheme. When it shows something other than the draft, a notice under **Preview** says what and why: there is no saved draft yet, a layer could not be read or names an image that is gone, a stylesheet was left out, or a template was refused or could not be filled and the page uses the one below it or the default. The notice is the server's own account of the same page, and is not shown when the frame is simply the draft. **Publish** makes the saved draft the published theme and keeps the version it replaces, up to twenty. **Versions** lists them; **Restore** publishes a copy of an earlier one and leaves the draft alone.

The **Publish** bar sits above every tab, so a change made on **Pages** is published from **Pages**. It says which version people see and what the draft would change, such as "Changed: Colours and font, Page templates", and a dot on a tab says that part of that change is in it. Publishing is always the whole draft: a template may name an image that only the draft has, the contrast pairs span the colours and the stylesheet, and a version is a whole snapshot. When nothing differs, **Publish** is held with "Nothing to publish: the draft matches what people see", and the server refuses the same publish if it is sent anyway.

**Revert this tab to published**, under **Revert to published** on **Theme** and **Pages**, writes the draft again with that tab's parts as they are published, and leaves the other tab's changes alone: **Theme** takes back the colours and font, the images and the stylesheet, and **Pages** the templates. A part with nothing published is cleared. It is held while the tab holds edits you have not saved. **Discard draft** deletes the saved draft after a confirmation and nothing else: what is published stays, and people keep seeing it.

## 4. See the mail

The **Mail** tab shows the address-verification mail as the published theme dresses it and the tenant's message overrides word it, in the light or the dark scheme and in a language you choose, drawn by the code that sends it. Beside it, **What the mail inherits** lists the logo (drawn when it is served from the console's own address, and otherwise pointed to in the preview) and the nine colours of each scheme (Page background, **Message background**, **Link box background**, Border, Text, **Quiet text**, Link, Button and **Button text**) and says whether the tenant's published theme or Odudu's default supplies each palette. The preview and this list show the tenant's own look until you choose a client under **Preview as client**: then the frame and the colours are those of the mail a sign-in of that client sends, each palette saying whether the client's theme, the tenant's or Odudu's default supplies it, and **Show the tenant’s own** goes back. Choosing a client needs `manage-clients`. A mail takes a client's logo and colours, over the tenant's piece by piece, only when the flow began inside that client's sign-in (a registration or a password reset asked for from it); otherwise it takes the tenant's. It never takes a client's stylesheet, templates or wording, and a draft does not show until it is published.

## 5. Choose the languages

On the **Languages** tab, **Supported languages** is the list of languages the tenant's pages and mail are offered in, and **Default language** is the one used when a visitor's browser does not decide. Choose "The browser decides" to clear the default. To offer another language, type its tag or pick it under Add a language, then press Enter or choose **Add language**, and save; its pages use the English text unless a message override says otherwise. Under **Message overrides**, pick a language and set the text a page or a mail shows for a message key such as `login.title` or `reset.subject`; **Message key** lists the keys the pages and the mail hold with their English text, the mail's marked "Mail", and a key you type that the catalogue does not hold is refused, and a tenant holds at most 500 overrides across every language. Edit an override by choosing **Edit** on its row, and remove it with **Remove**. Overrides change pages and mail, in the language each is shown in; overrides are the tenant's alone, and a client has none of its own.

## Things that cannot be undone

**Reset to default** deletes the draft and unpublishes the theme, so people see Instrument's own pages again; to drop only the draft, use **Discard draft**. The version that was published stays under **Versions**. **Delete image** removes a stored image, and is refused while any theme still names it.

## Do the same through the API

The calls are in the [theme](../reference/generated/admin-api/theme/index.md) area of the admin API: reading and saving the draft (the read says which parts of the draft differ from the published theme), publishing, discarding the draft with `DELETE /theme/draft`, versions and restore, reset, image upload, read and delete (`GET /theme/assets/{assetId}/content` answers the stored bytes the thumbnails show), and the preview, with `GET /theme/preview/info` saying what that preview shows and why. `GET /theme/stylesheet-hooks` lists what a stylesheet may name, which the stylesheet editor shows as its selectors. The mail preview and what the mail inherits are `GET /mail/preview` and `GET /mail/branding`; the overrides are the `/message-overrides` routes, and the keys offered are `GET /message-catalogue`.
