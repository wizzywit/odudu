# Subjects in the console

A subject is anything that signs in to a tenant: a person or a service. This is where you add one, help one who is locked out, and take one away. It needs the `view-users` capability to read and `manage-users` to change; the menu entry is **Subjects** under **IDENTITY**.

<ConsoleShot
  name="subjects"
  alt="The demo tenant's Subjects list, with the person ada and a Create a subject button."

/>

## 1. Find a subject

Open **Subjects**. The list shows the **Username**, **Email**, status and creation time of each subject, a page at a time. Search by the start of a **Username** or an **Email**, or narrow the list by **Status** and by the **Capability** a subject holds. The default, **Holding anything or nothing**, shows everyone. Opening a row opens the subject's record.

## 2. Create a subject

Choose **Create a subject**. A person needs only a **Username**; an **Email** is optional and can be added later. Then choose **Create subject**.

<ConsoleShot
  name="new-subject"
  alt="The Create a subject form with the username ada typed in."
  caption="A person needs only a username. There is no password field."
/>

There is no password field, on purpose. Nobody who administers Odudu chooses another person's password, so the next step gives the person a way to set their own.

## 3. Give them a first password

Open the subject, then the **Credentials** tab. You have two ways, and the tab explains which applies.

1. If the subject has an email address, choose **Send a password reset email**. The person chooses a password from their own mailbox. The tenant needs a [mail relay](./email.mdx) for the mail to leave the server.
2. Otherwise choose **Issue a one-time password**. The console asks you to confirm with **Issue password**, shows you a password once, and the person must change it at their next sign-in.

<ConsoleShot
  name="issue-password"
  alt="The Credentials tab of ada, with a dialog asking whether to issue a one-time password."
  caption="The console issues a one-time password. It is shown once, and the person changes it at first sign-in."
/>

Copy the password before you close the dialog, because it cannot be shown again. Issuing another replaces the first.

## 4. Look through the record

A subject's record has a tab for each part of an account.

<ConsoleShot
  name="subject-profile"
  alt="ada's record on its Profile tab, with the tabs Profile, Credentials, Groups, Roles, Required actions, Sessions, Consents and Grants above the account fields."
  caption="A person opens on a Profile tab. The Activity tab is just beyond the right edge."
/>

- **Profile** holds the account, the name, the details, the address and whether the email and phone number are verified. A section saves on its own with its own **Save** button.
  The name fields are **Full name**, **Given name**, **Family name**, **Middle name**, **Nickname** and **Preferred username**, which is what the person likes to be called and is not the sign-in username. The details are **Gender**, **Birthdate**, **Time zone**, **Locale**, **Profile page**, **Website**, **Picture** and **Phone number**, with **Phone number verified** beside it. The address is **Formatted address**, **Street**, **Locality**, **Region**, **Postal code** and **Country**. An application receives the ones its scopes carry: `profile`, `email`, `phone` and `address`.
- **Credentials** holds the password, the second factors the person enrolled, their recovery codes and any lockout. When you revoke recovery codes, tick **Ask for a new set at next sign-in** to have the person given new ones.
- **Groups** lists the groups the person belongs to; the groups' roles come with them.
- **Roles** assigns roles to the person directly, and sets what they may do in the admin console under **Admin capabilities**.
- **Required actions** is what the next sign-in asks for first: **Choose a new password**, **Set up an authenticator app**, **Register a passkey** or **Generate recovery codes**. **Required actions to email** sends the person a link that takes them through the ones you tick, in order. The password is set from the link itself; a second factor is enrolled only after they sign in.
- **Sessions** lists the person's live sign-ins, each with the **Device** it was opened on. Sign-ins from one browser sit together with a count, and those with no device come last.
- **Consents** lists what the person agreed to share with each client.
- **Grants** lists the tokens that are still in force, never the tokens themselves.
- **Activity** lists every change made to the subject.

## Things that cannot be undone, or that end somebody's work

- **Delete** removes the subject with its credentials, sessions, grants, roles and group memberships. Disabling instead keeps everything and stops the person signing in. The profile has both, as **Disable ada** and **Delete ada** for a person called ada. A client's service account cannot be deleted here: delete the client instead.
- **End every session** on the Sessions tab signs the person out everywhere, and **End session** ends one.
- **Revoke grants** on the Grants tab ends a client's access for the person, including grants that outlive a session.
- **Revoke recovery codes** on the Credentials tab discards the person's whole set at once.
- Removing a second factor means the next sign-in no longer asks for it.
- A change that would leave a tenant with no enabled administrator is refused, so the last one cannot be disabled or deleted.

## Do the same through the API

Every action here is a call in the [subjects area of the admin API](../reference/generated/admin-api/subjects/index.md), or in its [sign-in](../reference/generated/admin-api/subject-sign-in/index.md), [access](../reference/generated/admin-api/subject-access/index.md) and [sessions](../reference/generated/admin-api/subject-sessions/index.md) areas. Clearing the lockouts of a whole tenant is in [lockouts](../reference/generated/admin-api/lockouts/index.md). To choose a password rather than issue one, the command line has `odudu seed user --password`; the [roadmap](../roadmap.md) records why the console does not.
