# Roles and groups

Ada joins the backend team. On her first day she should be able to review code, and because she is on a team inside engineering she should also get whatever every engineer gets. Nobody wants to tick those boxes person by person. Roles and groups are how you say it once.

## Roles

A role is a name for something a person may do, such as `reviewer` or `billing-admin`. Odudu does not decide what the role allows. Your app does, when it reads the role from a token. A role can be:

- a **tenant role**, which any client in the tenant may read;
- a **client role**, named for one client and relevant only to it;
- a **composite role**, which bundles other roles so that holding it means holding them all.

A role can also be marked as a default for new people, so everyone created afterwards starts with it.

## Groups

A group is a place people belong. Groups form a tree, and each has a path: `/engineering` is a group, and `/engineering/backend` is a group inside it. A person can belong to several groups.

A group can be given roles. Everyone in the group holds them.

## How a person ends up with a role

There are three ways, and they add together:

1. The role is given to the person directly.
2. The role is given to a group the person belongs to.
3. The role is given to a group **above** the person's own. A role mapped to `/engineering` reaches everyone in `/engineering` and in every group beneath it.

The third is the one that saves work. Ada belongs to `/engineering/backend` and nothing else. She holds what backend gives her, and what engineering gives her too, though nobody ever added her to `/engineering` itself. Ask the admin API for her `effective-roles` and you see the full answer.

## What reaches a token

Two claims carry this to your app, and they do not agree on purpose.

- The **`roles` claim** lists every role the person holds, including those that came from groups above. Ada's lists both `reviewer` and `engineering-lead`. A role that belongs to one client appears with the client's id in front, as in `reports-api:reader`.
- The **`groups` claim** lists only the groups the person belongs to directly. Ada's lists `/engineering/backend` and nothing else.

If your app checks `groups` for `/engineering`, it will not find Ada, even though she holds everything engineering grants. Check roles, and your app agrees with what Odudu allows. Use `groups` when you want the person's own memberships, for example to show where they sit.

A role also reaches a token only when a scope carries it. You map the role to a scope, such as `roles`, and the claim appears when the client was granted that scope. A client with **Include all roles in tokens** on skips the mapping, but still needs the `roles` scope to get the claim. [Scopes](./scopes.mdx) explains the rule.

## Working with roles and groups

1. Decide which things your app needs to tell people apart by, and name one role for each.
2. Create the roles, then the groups that match how people are organised.
3. Give each group the roles its members should hold.
4. Put people in groups. Prefer a group to a direct role: one change then covers everybody in it.
5. Map the roles to a scope and give the client that scope, so they reach the token. Or turn on **Include all roles in tokens** and give the client the `roles` scope.
6. In your app, read `roles` from the access token. The access token carries it by default, and the ID token does not.

An administrator cannot put a person in a group whose roles reach further than their own, so nobody can use the admin API to grant more than they hold.
